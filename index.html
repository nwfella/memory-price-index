Method & limits

Retail series are asking prices, not transactions. RamRadar records the lowest live listing per kit, normalised to $/GB; eBay listings are filtered to new condition, Newegg/B&H are taken as listed. Coverage is uneven — eBay dominates the basket, and kit counts ship with every point (fewer than ~20 kits early in the window).

Spot and contract are different markets. Spot is a small, volatile, broker-driven market for loose chips; contract is what OEMs pay under quarterly agreements. Spot moves first and overshoots; retail sits downstream of both and lags by weeks to months. A falling 512Gb TLC wafer quote is a leading signal for SSD pricing, not a promise that a specific drive got cheaper today.

Annotated steps are composition, not negotiation. The vertical marker in May 2026 on the retail lines is a listing-mix change in the upstream index — the cheapest tracked DDR5 kit jumped from $3.17 to $6.77/GB as cheap listings aged out, not a 26% overnight price cut. Days with fewer than 10 tracked kits are excluded entirely.

Gaps are real. TrendForce does not publish every benchmark every week, and several weekly posts carry only one of the two figures — chart lines connect the observations that exist and never interpolate a missing week. Data is baked into this page at build time (2026-11-04); nothing is fetched from your browser.
